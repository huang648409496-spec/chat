Dual-Port USB & HDMI Panel Mount Adapters for Space-Limited Control Cabinets and HMI Panels

Dual-port panel mount adapters combine two interfaces—USB-A, USB-C or HDMI—into one compact module. One cutout instead of two. Less machining, cleaner layout, faster assembly. This guide covers the five common combinations, how to specify them, and what to confirm before ordering.

TL;DR - Key Takeaways
  • Dual-port panel mount adapters combine two interfaces into one module, requiring only one panel cutout instead of two.
  • Eliminates the clearance gap normally needed between two separate single-port cutouts, recovering usable panel area.
  • Five common combinations: Dual USB-A, Dual USB-C, USB-A + USB-C, HDMI + USB-A, HDMI + USB-C.
  • Suitable for control cabinets, HMI panels, industrial PCs, kiosks and OEM equipment with limited front panel area.
  • For quotation, provide: port combination, panel cutout dimensions, panel thickness, cable length and USB/HDMI version.

Definition: A dual-port panel mount adapter is a single module that integrates two external interfaces (USB, USB-C or HDMI) into one panel-mounted housing, allowing engineers to provide two connection points through one cutout position on a control cabinet or equipment panel.

The Problem: Front Panel Space Is Fixed, Interface Demands Are Not

Control cabinets and HMI panels have fixed front panel area. Once the display, push buttons, emergency stop, indicator lights and cable glands are placed, the remaining space for external service ports is often limited to a small strip or corner.

Meanwhile, modern industrial equipment increasingly needs multiple external interfaces in the same service area. An HMI system may need HDMI for an external monitor plus USB for a keyboard during configuration. A control cabinet may need USB-A for legacy maintenance tools plus USB-C for newer laptops. A test instrument may need two USB ports for simultaneous data logging and firmware update.

When each interface uses a separate single-port adapter, the result is multiple cutouts, multiple mounting positions and a crowded panel layout. For equipment produced in batches, this also multiplies machining, assembly and QC cost.

Single-Port vs Dual-Port: What Changes in Practice

The space saving is not just the cutout itself. The minimum clearance between two adjacent cutouts—needed to maintain panel rigidity—is eliminated entirely with a dual-port module.

Item Two Single-Port Adapters One Dual-Port Adapter
Panel cutouts required 2 separate openings 1 combined opening
Total panel area used Larger (includes inter-cutout clearance) Smaller (clearance eliminated)
Screw positions 4 (2 per adapter) 2
Internal cables 2 separate tails 1 or 2 tails from one module
Assembly steps Mount, align, fix × 2 Mount, align, fix × 1
Panel appearance Two separate openings One clean integrated opening

Five Common Dual-Port Combinations

Dual USB-A

Two USB-A ports in one panel module. Suitable for equipment that uses legacy USB peripherals: USB drives, keyboards, mice, barcode scanners, maintenance laptops. USB version options: USB 2.0 or USB 3.0. For most industrial maintenance tasks (firmware upload, log download), USB 2.0 is sufficient. USB 3.0 is preferred when large data files are transferred regularly.

Dual USB-C

Two USB-C ports in one module. Suitable for newer equipment designs using Type-C for data transfer, charging or both.

⚠️ Important: USB-C is a connector form factor, not a single protocol. Before ordering, confirm whether each port needs to support data only, data + charging, or data + video (Alt Mode). Not all USB-C panel adapters support all functions.

USB-A + USB-C

One USB-A and one USB-C in one module. A practical transition-period combination for equipment that must serve both legacy USB-A devices and newer Type-C devices. This is currently the most common request from OEM equipment manufacturers because it covers both existing maintenance tools and newer laptops without requiring users to carry adapters.

HDMI + USB-A

One HDMI and one USB-A in one module. Suitable for HMI panels, embedded PCs and control systems that need display output + peripheral access from the front panel. When specifying, confirm the HDMI version (1.4 for 1080p, 2.0 for 4K @ 60 Hz) and whether an active cable is needed for longer rear cable runs.

HDMI + USB-C

One HDMI and one USB-C in one module. Suitable for newer compact systems where the operator needs both external display and Type-C data or charging access from one service panel area.

Mechanical Specifications to Confirm Before Ordering

Dual-port adapters require more careful dimension checking than single-port versions because the panel cutout is larger and rear clearance is more critical.

Must-Confirm Items

  1. Port combination. Which two interfaces are needed?
  2. Panel cutout dimensions. The rectangular opening size in the metal panel.
  3. Panel thickness range. Confirm whether the adapter supports the actual enclosure thickness; thicker panels may require extended-thread variants.
  4. Rear cable length and direction. How much space is available behind the panel? Does the cable need to exit straight, at 90° or at a specific angle?
  5. USB / HDMI version. This determines the internal wiring and connector spec.

📩 Tip for OEM Projects

Providing a panel drawing with internal clearance dimensions is the fastest way to confirm fit. This eliminates back-and-forth on cutout tolerances and rear cable routing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the two ports in a dual-port adapter have different cable lengths?

Yes. Custom versions can have different rear cable lengths for each port. This is common when the two internal connection points are at different distances behind the panel.

What panel thickness do dual-port adapters typically support?

Most standard versions support common industrial panel and enclosure thicknesses. For thicker enclosures, extended-thread or clamp-mount variants are available. Confirm with the supplier if panel thickness is outside the standard range.

Are dual-port adapters available in IP65-rated versions?

Standard versions are typically IP20 (indoor, no splash). IP65/IP67 versions with gaskets and sealed caps are available for wash-down or outdoor environments, but must be specified at quotation stage.

Does USB-C on a panel adapter support charging and video?

Not necessarily. USB-C is a connector form factor. Whether a specific panel adapter supports data only, data + charging, or data + video (Alt Mode) depends on the internal wiring. Always confirm the required function before ordering.

What happens to HDMI signal quality on longer rear cable runs?

For HDMI at higher resolutions, passive cables have a practical length limit beyond which signal may degrade. If the rear cable run is long, confirm with the supplier whether an active cable or signal conditioner is needed.
